[Heading1] WhatsApp Survey Bot — PRD
[] Sep 20, 2026 · @Someone
[Heading2] Overview
[] This product lets anyone run a survey inside WhatsApp, so people answer one question at a time in a chat instead of opening a Google Form.
[] A creator signs up on the website, builds a survey, publishes it, and shares the bot's WhatsApp contact or link with their audience. When a respondent taps Start, the bot asks the questions in turn, and every answer lands in the creator's dashboard. If a respondent stops halfway, the bot sends a reminder.
[] The UI, core functionality and super admin controls are already built. This PRD defines what "finished" means so the full-stack developer can close the remaining gaps and harden the product for launch, while the founder focuses on marketing and distribution.
[Quote] Working title: "WhatsApp Survey Bot". Replace with the final product name.
[Heading2] Problem
[] Most people never finish a survey that makes them leave WhatsApp, and the ones who do are pulled out of what they were doing.
[] Today a survey sent over WhatsApp means: tap a link, wait for a browser, load a form, fill it in, then find your way back to the chat. Each step loses people. The interruption also breaks the person's flow on their phone, so many simply ignore the message. Creators end up with low response rates and no easy way to nudge the people who meant to answer.
[] The conversation already happens in WhatsApp. The survey should happen there too: tap Start, tap an answer, done in about 30 seconds, with the bot reminding people who forget.
[Heading2] Goals and non-goals
[] The goal of this release is a product that a creator can use end to end, and a respondent can finish, without anyone fixing things in the background.
[] Goals
[ListParagraph] A respondent completes a survey entirely inside WhatsApp, with no account and no browser.
[ListParagraph] A creator can sign up, build, publish, share and read results without help from the team.
[ListParagraph] Good UI
[ListParagraph] Reminders lift completion without annoying people.
[ListParagraph] Responses are recorded accurately: no lost answers, no duplicates, correct completion status.
[ListParagraph] The product is stable enough for a real pilot with an outside organization.
[] Non-goals for this release
[ListParagraph] Channels other than WhatsApp.
[ListParagraph] Advanced analytics such as cross-tabulation (see Later).
[ListParagraph] Branching or skip logic (see Later).

[Heading2] Reminders
[] Reminders are what make this more than a Google Form inside WhatsApp, and also the easiest way to annoy people, so they need firm limits.
[] Proposed rules (defaults the creator can change per survey)
[ListParagraph] Send reminders only to respondents who started but did not finish.
[ListParagraph] Maximum 2 reminders per respondent per survey.
[ListParagraph] Wait at least several hours before the first reminder and about a day before the second.
[ListParagraph] Send only during daytime hours, proposed 8:00 to 20:00 West Africa Time.
[ListParagraph] Say how many questions remain, for example "You have 3 questions left. It takes under a minute."
[ListParagraph] Stop immediately when the respondent completes, replies STOP, or the survey closes.
[ListParagraph] Creators can turn reminders off for a survey.
[] Example reminder
[Code] Hi! You haven't finished {Creator name}'s survey yet.You have 3 questions left, about 30 seconds.Reply CONTINUE to pick up where you stopped, or STOP to opt out.
[] Technical constraint the developer must design around
[] WhatsApp only allows free-form messages within 24 hours of the respondent's last message. Reminders sent after that window must use a pre-approved message template, which Meta reviews and usually charges for. This means the second reminder (and any reminder after a day) needs an approved template, and message cost becomes a real cost of the product. Confirm the current rules and pricing in Meta's documentation before finalizing the cadence.
[] Done when: reminders fire on schedule, never exceed the cap, respect opt-out and quiet hours, and resume the survey at the right question.

[Heading2] WhatsApp platform constraints
[] These are the WhatsApp rules that shape the design. They are from general knowledge of the WhatsApp Business Platform and change over time, so the developer must verify each against Meta's current documentation.
[ListParagraph] Official API only. The bot must run on the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) with a business number. Automating a normal personal WhatsApp account with unofficial tools breaks WhatsApp's terms and risks the number being banned.
[ListParagraph] One number, many surveys. If all surveys share one bot number, forwarding the contact alone cannot say which survey the respondent wants. Each survey needs a click-to-chat link with a survey code in the pre-filled message (for example "START ABC123"), or a code the respondent types. Confirm how the current build routes this.
[ListParagraph] Interactive message limits. Reply buttons are limited to 3 options, and list messages to 10 rows, each with short titles. This drives the rendering rules in the respondent section.
[ListParagraph] 24-hour window. Free-form replies are allowed only within 24 hours of the respondent's last message. Anything later, including most reminders, needs an approved message template.
[ListParagraph] Opt-in and opt-out. Respondents opt in by messaging the bot first. Every reminder must offer a way to stop, and STOP must be honored immediately.
[ListParagraph] Quality and sending limits. Reports and blocks by recipients can lower the number's quality rating and reduce how many messages it may send. This is why reminder caps matter.
[ListParagraph] Business setup. Meta may require business verification and display-name approval before the number can be used at scale.
[ListParagraph] Webhook reliability. Incoming messages can arrive twice or out of order. Deduplicate by WhatsApp message ID and keep a stored conversation state per respondent, so a restart or retry never loses or repeats an answer.
[Heading2] Non-functional requirements
[] The product handles people's phone numbers and answers, so privacy, security and reliability are launch requirements, not extras.
[] Privacy and data handling
[ListParagraph] Collect only what is needed: the WhatsApp number and the answers.
[ListParagraph] Tell respondents in the opening message who is collecting the data, and link to a privacy notice.
[ListParagraph] Creators own their survey data; the platform must not share it with other creators.
[ListParagraph] Support deletion of a respondent's data on request, and define a retention period.
[ListParagraph] Comply with the Nigeria Data Protection Act. Get legal review of the privacy notice and terms before launch.
[] Security and abuse protection
[ListParagraph] Encrypt data in transit and at rest; protect the dashboard and admin panel with authentication and role checks.
[ListParagraph] Rate-limit account creation, survey creation and bot messages.
[ListParagraph] Creators accept terms that ban spam, harassment and phishing surveys; admins can take down offenders.
[ListParagraph] Respondents can report a survey (for example by replying REPORT) so admins see it.
[ListParagraph] Protect the bot's webhook: verify that requests really come from WhatsApp.
[] Reliability and quality
[ListParagraph] The bot replies quickly enough to feel like a chat. Proposed target: a reply within 3 seconds for 95% of messages.
[ListParagraph] Answers already given are never lost, even if a step fails.
[ListParagraph] Errors show friendly messages to respondents and are logged for admins.
[ListParagraph] The website and dashboard work on phone screens.

[] End-to-end test
[] The developer runs both journeys on real phones: creator (sign up, create, publish, share, receive responses, analyze) and respondent (receive, start, answer, finish). Also test: a respondent who stops midway and gets reminded, one who replies STOP, one who sends invalid answers, and two people answering at the same moment.
[] Pilot
[] Before approaching organizations, run one real 5-question survey with 20 to 50 people from the founder's own network. Measure how many started, how many completed, how long it took, where people dropped off, whether any question was misunderstood, whether WhatsApp delivery and reminders worked, and whether the dashboard recorded everything accurately. Then repeat with one outside organization.
[Heading2] Risks and open questions
[] These need a decision from the founder or the developer before or during the finish work.
[ListParagraph] ☐ Product name. What is the final name, and does the bot display it or the creator's name?
[ListParagraph] ☐ WhatsApp setup. Is the bot on the official Cloud API with a verified business number, or something else? Who pays for template messages?
[ListParagraph] ☐ Routing. How does a shared bot number know which survey a respondent wants (link with code, typed code, or a number per survey)?
[ListParagraph] ☐ Anonymous or identified. Can creators see respondents' phone numbers, or only answers? Should this be a per-survey setting?
[ListParagraph] ☐ Editing after publish. What may a creator change once responses exist (fix typos only, add questions, reorder)?
[ListParagraph] ☐ Reminder cadence. Confirm the defaults (2 reminders, daytime only) against Meta's template rules and costs.
[ListParagraph] ☐ Language. Is the launch English only, or does the bot need Pidgin or other languages?
[ListParagraph] ☐ Data retention. How long are responses and phone numbers kept, and who can request deletion?
[ListParagraph] ☐ Pricing and limits. Is the launch free, and what per-creator quotas apply?
[] Main risks
[ListParagraph] Message costs and Meta template approval can slow or raise the cost of reminders.
[ListParagraph] Too many reminders or unwanted messages can get the number flagged and its sending limits reduced.
[ListParagraph] Abuse (spam or phishing surveys) can damage the bot's reputation with WhatsApp, so takedown must be fast.
